The Practice

I always seem to land somewhere in the middle.

Not quite a marketer. Not quite a designer. Not quite an event planner, though I have built a tablescape or twelve. I’m an analytical creative, a daydreamer and a doer, and most of what I do sits in the space between those categories, which is exactly where the good ideas tend to hide.

How People Find Me

It usually starts with a text.

“

Can you Photoshop this for me quick?

“

What color should I paint my walls?

“

I saw this thing and I want to do something like it. Where do I even start?

“

Will you make the playlist for the party bus?

“

Help me plan my son’s birthday party.

“

You should write a book.

None of those are technically a job description. But they are, somehow, the job. Somewhere along the way I stopped trying to narrow it down to one lane and started calling it a practice instead, because that word already covers the range this work actually requires.

How I Work

Three ways in, depending on what you need.

I can help you think an idea through, or I can take it off your plate entirely. I can teach you how to do something, work alongside you while you do it, or disappear into the details and come back when it’s finished. Some people want to know exactly how the sausage is made. Some would strongly prefer not to. Both are fine with me. Tell me which kind of help you’re after and the rest of the process follows from there.

What This Actually Is

Not a service menu. A practice.

The practice covers marketing, branding, web and digital work, and event and occasion planning, whatever a project actually calls for, rather than whatever fits neatly on a price sheet. Most of what lands on my desk needs more than one of those at once, so I stopped separating them into different businesses and started treating them as one practice with a few different lanes.

Background

The design instincts come from years of working inside brands before I started building this one. The rest, apparently, just comes naturally.

“No idea is too big. No detail is too small.”
