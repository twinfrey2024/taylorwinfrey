Event Notes · Kearney, NE

Wine, diamonds and a proposal someone else plans.

I’m working with Hawthorne Jewelry, a family store on the Bricks in downtown Kearney since 1878, on two new ways to bring people through the door.

Package One

Wine & Learn: all things diamonds

Hawthorne has always sold on education. People come in wondering why two stones of the same size carry very different prices, and they leave knowing how to read a grading report. Wine & Learn gives that conversation an evening of its own. It’s a small ticketed night in the store, capped around 12 to 20 guests so everyone gets time at the loupe.

The team walks guests through cut, color, clarity and carat weight with loose stones, then sets natural and lab grown diamonds side by side. The last stretch of the night is open questions and browsing. A local winery handles the pour, which keeps costs down and puts the event in front of their audience too.

The ticket price comes back as store credit within a set window. That one decision turns a fun night into something the store can measure, because every attendee who buys shows up in the numbers.

Before announcing any event like this, check how alcohol can be served in a retail space under your state’s rules. A free pour and a ticketed pour can fall under different permits.

Package Two

The Proposal Package

Most people feel ready to pick a ring. Planning the moment around it is where the nerves set in. With this package, the buyer chooses a ring at Hawthorne and adds a flat planning fee, and the store handles the arrangements.

That means booking the reservation and placing a photographer nearby before the couple arrives. The store can also deliver the ring to the venue ahead of time, so nobody spends dinner patting their jacket pocket. Two tiers keep it simple to sell. The essentials tier covers the reservation and ring delivery, and the full tier adds the photographer plus extras like flowers or a private room.

Every plan starts with a conversation about the person being asked. A quiet dinner for two and a family surprise in the next room call for very different evenings.

How it runs

  1. 01

    The buyer chooses a ring, from the case or a custom design.

  2. 02

    A short sit-down covers the partner’s style and the moment the buyer pictures.

  3. 03

    The store books vetted partners at agreed rates and sends one confirmed plan.

  4. 04

    The buyer shows up and asks. Photos arrive afterward.

Behind the scenes, the package needs written rates from each restaurant and photographer, a clear policy on who carries the ring during delivery and how it’s insured, and a minimum lead time so the team never scrambles.

Why Both

One brings them in early. The other closes the loop.

Wine & Learn reaches people months before they’re ready to buy and gives them a reason to trust one store over the rest. The Proposal Package gives them a reason to buy there once they are. Launching both ahead of the holiday season lines them up with the busiest stretch of the year for proposals.
